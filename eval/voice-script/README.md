# Does the voice carry the conversation as designed, without the glasses

This study checks the voice's prompt (`gpt/prompts.py`, `VOICE_PERSONA`) against GPT-Live itself on a scripted build conversation, with no glasses, brain or camera, so a prompt change can be judged at the desk in two minutes instead of in a lab run. It was built on the evening of 2026-11-03, after the second lab run of the day showed the voice confirming the right brick without saying where it goes, giving the next brick without saying the step was done, and handing over to the brain although the brain's look had been removed that afternoon.

## Material

The scripts in `scripts/` are the wearer's lines and the brain's lines of a ladder build as the lab call of 2026-11-03 17:00 (`logs/20261103-170016-glasses-f6ccf01d`) went: the wearer says they are ready, the brain says they hold the right brick, they say they placed it, the progress moves, they ask whether the step is done and where the next brick goes, the brain finds a brick misplaced and offers a picture, they accept it. `ladder-smoke.json` is the first four exchanges, `ladder-pilot.json` the whole call.

## Method

`run.py <script>` runs livekit-agents' `AgentSession` with the GPT-Live plugin exactly as the agent does (client delegation, no VAD, the same persona and the same greeting request), but in place of the room's audio it attaches two ends of its own: a microphone that sends the script's lines as 24 kHz audio made once by OpenAI TTS (`gpt-4o-mini-tts`, cached in `out/tts/`), as 20 ms frames at wall-clock pace with silence between, and a speaker that plays nothing but lets each segment of the voice's audio run its own length on the wall clock before reporting it played, so the session's speaking state keeps real time. The brain's lines go in as the brain sends them, `append_commentary` for a say or a progress move, `append_thinking` for a tell. A hand-over GPT-Live makes is closed as `brain.py` closes it, with `HANDED_BACK`. Each step waits until the voice has answered and been quiet for a second, or 20 s, and can carry regular expressions the voice's words must match or must not. The run writes `out/<time>-<script>/`: `events.jsonl`, `report.md` with the dialogue, each voice turn's seconds and words, the latency from the wearer's last word to the answer, the expectations met and the totals, and `dialogue.wav` with the voice on the left and the wearer on the right, to hear the pace.

The first run showed two things about the harness itself, fixed before any result was read: the session's "speaking" state follows the speaker's `playback_started` report, so without it every step ran to its timeout; and the voice's turn times in the chat history come with room audio only, so the report takes them from the state changes.

## Result (2026-11-03 evening)

With the prompt as deployed at 17:00 (commit 20b76ec) the smoke script reproduced the lab run's faults: after the brain said the brick was right the voice said only "Good, that's the right brick"; after "I put it down" it handed over, the closing line "answer from what the helper has already told you" made it say "Checking. Find the white one by six brick next", and after the progress moved it said "You're on step two", with no word that the step was done and no brick named.

Six changes to the voice's prompt, and two to the closing line, were then tested on the same scripts, one run at a time. The voice's persona tells it that it has nothing to hand over to and never waits for a reply; that a "Progress:" line means the step is done, to be said in one breath with the next brick ("That's step one. Now the white one by six."); that after "I placed it" it says a few words and stops until a "Progress:" line counts the step; and that what the helper has already said it answers at once and plainly ("Yes, that's the right one", "Yes, step one is done"), since without that sentence it answered "One moment" and "That's step one" to those questions in one run of three. Two more sentences came from later runs: when the brain says the wearer holds the step's brick, the voice confirms it and says where it goes in the same breath, which one run of three had skipped; and when the progress says the build is finished, the voice says so and that the task is over, where it had said "That's the last step". The hand-over's closing line went through two wordings: "still looking at it; the helper says when it has checked" made the voice say "I'm still looking" to a question it had the answer to, so it now repeats the brain's last line (`brain.handed_back`: "Camera: nothing new since the last look; still: ..."), which the voice answers from when it covers the question. GPT-Live still hands over now and then whatever the prompt says, 0 to 4 times a run. The smoke script then met 5 of 5 expectations and the whole call, with a finishing step added, 18 of 18 on its last run (14 to 17 on the runs before: the voice split one answer into two sentences a second apart and the script moved on between them, one of the wearer's lines was sent while the voice still spoke and GPT-Live heard only its last word, one expression did not allow for "That's step two", and the answers the later changes are for).

On 2026-11-04 two exchanges joined the whole-call script after a 0.25 Mbps lab run: a brain line that cannot tell yet ("looks like a tan brick, but I can't count its studs; turn them toward me"), which the voice had cut down to "just turn the studs toward me" and the wearer heard as an order out of nowhere, now expected to carry both what the brain sees and what it asks; and a hedged line ("looks orange to me, but I may be mistaken"), expected to stay hedged and never become "that's wrong". With the two rules added to the persona the script met 22 of 22.

## The instructor persona (2026-11-04 evening)

The persona was rewritten as the instructor of a video call, after OpenAI's Prompting GPT-Live template (a backchannel policy, an interruption policy and a delegation policy written with the token `<|SpawnThinking|>`), and the scripts with it: a step is given as brick and place together, "is this the right one" and "done" get "let me see" and nothing more, "okay" gets no sentence, a colour the user names stands, and a helper line that says what is in the way ("your hand is still over it, lift it for a second") is passed on as a request. The smoke script met 9 of 9 and the whole call 35 of 36 on the first run each; the miss was an expectation that had not allowed "yep, that's the one" as the answer to "no, it's the yellow one", which is the behaviour wanted. The whole call's dialogue: "Tan one-by-six first, vertical, roughly in the middle of the plate, a little left of center." / "Let me see." / "Yep, that's the one." / "Good, that's step one. Now the white one-by-six, vertical, to the right of the tan with four empty columns between them." / "Step two: the white one-by-six goes vertical to the right of the tan..." / "White one's one column too far right; move it one left. Lift your hand for a second, so I can see." / "The one in your hand looks orange to me; the step needs the yellow one, or is that it?" / "Yep, that's the one." / "That's the build finished. What the run still showed, and the smoke runs after it settled: the voice repeated the place when it confirmed the brick in the hand ("Yep, that's the one; vertical, to the right of the tan...") although the step had given it, so the persona now answers "got it" with "good" and a confirmed brick with "Yep, that's the one." alone, which the next smoke run gave (1.9 s). The greeting ran 11 s and 40 words on "two or three short sentences" and 4.5 s and 15 words once the request was one sentence ("I see your table. We're doing LEGO ladder on a baseplate. Say when you're ready."), since the participant is briefed before the session and hears it once a round. One smoke run in five had the voice answer "I put it down" with nothing at all where the others said "Let me see" or "Mm-hm", so the rule now says "at once, every time, never with silence".

## Can a line cut into the voice's own turn (2026-11-05, after midnight)

Three scripts (`interrupt-commentary.json`, `interrupt-instructions.json`, `interrupt-both.json`) have the wearer ask for all five steps in detail, so the voice sets out on a long turn, and three seconds in send a line. A commentary alone ("Progress: 1 of 5 steps done...") did not cut in: the voice finished its sentence on step one, then wove the line in and dropped the rest of its plan ("...a little left of center. Good, that's step one. Now the white one-by-six..."), about three seconds after the append. An appended instruction ("Stop what you are saying at once, mid-sentence, and say only this: ...") made it break off mid-word within two seconds ("take the tan 1x6 Step | one is done, now the white one by six...") and say the new content. An instruction to stop followed at once by the commentary did the same and read more smoothly ("take the tan 1 by 6 and set Good, that's step one. Now the white..."). OpenAI's documentation says an appended instruction "can interrupt speech in progress"; this is that, used on purpose. The harness takes `{"instructions": "..."}` steps since.

What the project does with it: `gpt/eyes.py` precedes an urgent line, a step done or a brick placed wrong, with the stop instruction when the voice is speaking (`speak(..., urgent=True)`, a `cut_in` event in the record), and nothing else, since the cut is abrupt and the appended instructions stay in the model's context.

## What the project does with it

The prompt changes and the closing line are in `backend/agent/src/gpt/`, to be deployed and heard on the glasses on the next lab day. From now on a change to the voice's prompt is run through `ladder-pilot.json` before it goes to the glasses; a behaviour seen wrong in a lab run becomes a step with an expectation. What this harness cannot show: how the brain's own lines come and when, since the brain is scripted here; the brain's rules are a matter for the records and for `brain-thread-replay/`.
