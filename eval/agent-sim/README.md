# Does the whole agent hold together without the glasses

This harness runs the GPT path's agent as it runs in a call, GPT-Live, the eyes and the consult together, with a recorded call standing in for the room, so that a change to the structure can be seen working before the glasses are in reach. It was built on the evening of 2026-11-04, when the lab PC was out of reach from home and the day's structure had been tested only one part at a time.

## Question

Do the parts run together as designed: who speaks when, how long after a frame the eyes' line and the consult's sentence reach the voice, whether the eyes and the consult contradict each other, and what a minute costs?

## Material

A recorded call's record (`logs/<call>/`): its photos, each the frame the brain was sent at the second it was taken (one every two seconds), and the wearer's lines with the second each was said. The snowman call of 2026-11-04 16:44 (`20261104-164445-glasses-ef8ed836`, a clean build, the lime 1x4 read as yellow) and the gate call of 2026-10-28 16:23 (`20261028-162315-glasses-795c9128`, the white 1x6 one column too far right from 245 s on) are the two used.

## Method

`sim.py <record> [--from 240 --until 300] [--quick-every 2] [--no-consult] [--photos]` starts livekit-agents' `AgentSession` with the GPT-Live plugin exactly as `gpt/agent.py` does (client delegation, the persona, the greeting request), then the eyes (`gpt/eyes.py`) on the Decisions API and the consult (`gpt/consult.py`) on the brain's model over the WebSocket, with the call's record (`runlog.py`) written as in a call. The room is stood in for: the microphone plays the wearer's recorded lines as TTS audio (`eval/voice-script`'s `ScriptMic` and `tts`) at the second each was said; the speaker keeps the voice's audio and its timing and plays nothing (`Ear`); the camera makes each of the record's photos the newest frame at the second it was taken, so the eyes see a new frame every two seconds instead of fifteen a second and read each once; the step list and the picture go into the record instead of to glasses. A record with a glasses recording (`deploy/glasses.ps1 -Record`: `recording/video.mp4` at 15 fps, `mic.wav`, each frame and chunk stamped with its wall clock) is played from the recording instead, the video decoded and turned upright frame by frame and the wearer's own voice fed to the microphone, so the eyes see the camera as on the device and GPT-Live hears the real wearer; `--photos` asks for the photos all the same. `--from` begins partway in, with the progress the record had then. The wearer's lines were answers to another voice, so the dialogue is not judged for sense; the timing and the mechanics are.

`out/<time>-sim-<record>/` holds the record (`events.jsonl`, the consults' photos, the prompts), `agent.log`, `dialogue.wav` (left the voice, right the wearer) and `report.md`: the timeline of lines said, lines to the voice with their source (eyes, consult, fallback, hand-over), progress, words and how they were routed, consults; and the figures: the voice's share, the eyes' and the consults' latencies, how long after an eyes' or a consult's line the voice started, the progress and any reopening, hand-overs, failures.

Two runs more, after the consult switch (`BRAIN_CONSULT`, `--no-consult`) went in: with the consult off, the gate stretch read "That white one's not quite in place.", then after "I do it right" the place said again and "still not quite in its place", and after "How about now" "It's still not in its place."; with all three on, the committed code, "Hmm, not quite." and 2.1 s later "three columns too far right", but GPT-Live's transcript handed the wearer's turns over in pieces ("I I do it" / "right", "And one by" / "six") and each piece classified alone sent a consult that the next piece's cancelled, three wasted in a minute. That run's shutdown hung after the record's end event, in the session's close; each shutdown step now has 15 s.

### On a glasses recording (2026-11-04, late evening)

The ladder call of 2026-10-27 16:51 (`logs/20261027-165157-glasses-ad279efb/`, exposure -6, 155 s) has a recording, so it was played with its video at 15 fps and the wearer's own voice, the task as it was then (`--guide`, the light aqua 1x4 as the last brick, recovered from git: the task file changed to lime on 2026-10-28, and a first run against today's file judged the last step's colour against the wrong brick). GPT-Live transcribed the real voice right ("Yes, I'm ready", "Does it look good", "Uh, yeah, I put it down", "Ah, that might be a white one"); "Does it look good" went to the consult, which read the recorded misplacement as the call's own brain had ("the blue brick is three rows too low; move it up three rows"), and "How about now" had it record the step once the brick was moved. The decoder on this laptop kept 76 to 81 percent of the frames, the rest dropped to stay on time.

What the real frames showed that the photos had not: the eyes called a white brick in the last brick's place "as the step says" at 0.75 to 0.96 and finished the build on it, which in a lab call ends the task, so the step questions got a fourth, whether the brick at the step's place is the step's colour, the step rule needs it, and the last step is confirmed by the brain's model before the build is called finished (the consult on); the next run had the model answer "The bottom rung looks white rather than light aqua, so please swap it", the recorded moment. Under the dark exposure the eyes also read the white rail as not white and said "not quite" to a right placement, corrected by the consult in 2.5 s; a doubt about the colour alone, with the brick sitting right, now goes to the brain's model quietly, and "not quite" is kept for a brick that sits wrong. A declined finish is asked again after 10 s with the count still at the last step. The step questions had gone on to "step 6" after the build was finished and the model refused them; they stay on the last step.

The last run of the recording, with the urgent lines cutting in (`gpt/eyes.py` `speak(..., urgent=True)`, found on `eval/voice-script/`'s interrupt scripts): all five steps recorded, four by the eyes within a second of the hand leaving and the last by the consult after the white brick had been called out twice ("The bottom rung looks white rather than light aqua; replace it") and swapped; three cut-ins, the first turning "So, vertical, about mid left on the plate. Yep," into "Good, that's step one." 1.0 s after the progress; "Does it look good" answered "Not quite, the blue brick is three rows too low; move it up three rows." 3.2 s after the words; the voice at 40 percent, 10 words a turn. The run's one rough stretch had three lines in four seconds at the start of the last step, the eyes' progress, the consult's answer to "How about now" and the eyes' "not in its place yet" on the white brick, which the voice ran together.

A question about the desk had taken five seconds from the end of the words to the answer: 1.2 to 1.5 s for GPT-Live's transcript item, 1.2 s of waiting for a second piece of the turn, 0.3 s to classify, 1.1 to 1.9 s for the consult, 0.7 to 1.0 s for the voice. The waiting went: the first piece is classified and acted on at once and a later piece reclassifies the joined turn, undoing the first action only when the kind changed; and a question about the scene gets the eyes' own verdict from the latest reading as a first beat when that reading is plain. On the run after, "Does it look good" was answered "Hmm, not quite. It's three rows too low, so move it up three rows." 3.7 s after the words ended, "How about now" with the step recorded 3.7 s after, "I put it down" 4.0 s after; what remains is GPT-Live's own 1.5 s to the transcript item and 1 s to speech, and the consult's second. The same run had the voice make a helper line up ("Camera: the plate looks a little out of focus") and announce the build finished without a Progress line; the persona forbids both now.

Figures of the last runs: the eyes' readings 380 to 460 ms median, 2600 input tokens a frame (both requests); consults 1.0 to 2.9 s, the first of a call the slowest; the voice spoke 30 to 40 percent of the time at 8 to 12 words a turn; two Decisions requests in 150 came back 503 and were skipped.
