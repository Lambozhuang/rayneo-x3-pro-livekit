# Research brief: designing a LEGO-style build task that a vision-language model can verify step by step

## Background

We are building an AR-glasses assistant (RayNeo X3 Pro, monocular 1080p wide-angle camera on the
wearer's head, audio in/out, a small text display) that guides the wearer through a short LEGO
build. The assistant is a research workload: the real study is how network quality (latency, loss,
bandwidth) changes the user experience of such an assistant. The build task therefore has to be
verified almost perfectly by the model under good network conditions, otherwise the experiment
measures model error instead of network effects.

Current pipeline: the glasses stream video over WebRTC to a server; every 2-3 s one frame goes to a
vision-language model (OpenAI GPT-class, stateless call per frame) with the step list, the previous
verdict and a rendered reference image of what the build should look like at the current step; it
returns how many steps are visibly complete and whether a brick is placed wrongly. A voice model
tells the wearer the next step or the correction.

What we learned with a 4-step house (2x4 plate, 2x2 bricks, a 2x2 slope roof), built in the hands:
- colour and stud counting on a flat top-down layout works well;
- the 3D orientation of a small part (which way a slope roof faces) is unreliable at every model
  size and reasoning effort we tried, with or without cropping;
- "along which row / at which end" becomes ambiguous when the assembly is turned in the hands
  (no fixed reference frame);
- specular reflection on glossy bricks makes blue read as white;
- prompt priming: a brick placed in an L shape is seen when asked neutrally, missed when the
  question is embedded in the full step-checking prompt.

## What we want to find out

1. **Prior work on step-level verification of manual assembly from egocentric (head-mounted)
   video**: datasets, benchmarks and papers, whatever the assembled object is. How do they define
   "a step is complete"? What accuracy do they report, and which failure modes do they document?
2. **Vision-language-model-based progress tracking or mistake detection in manual tasks**
   (recent work, 2024 onwards): what made it work, what task simplifications were needed, and
   what limits are reported, in particular on judging the pose or orientation of small parts?
3. **AR/HCI user studies that used LEGO or similar block building as the task**: how the task
   was designed (number of steps, parts, where the build sits), how they scored errors and
   completion time, and why they chose what they chose.
4. **Task design under our constraints**: head-mounted wide-angle 1080p camera, one frame every
   2-3 s, hands in the picture, glossy bricks, a stateless VLM judge with a rendered reference
   image, a 5-10 minute session that should still expose network delay effects. Given what the
   literature says, what should such a task look like? Reason from the evidence you find, not
   from our current design; we want to be told if the whole approach is wrong.
5. **Network-QoE studies of AI assistants / conversational agents / AR guidance** that report how
   added latency or loss changed user ratings, so we can position our experiment.

## Output wanted

A structured note, English or Chinese, with sources (title, venue/year, link). Sections following
the five questions above, then a short "recommended task design" (a few concrete options with
pros/cons for a VLM judge) and a list of the most relevant papers to read first. Flag anything
where the evidence is thin or you are guessing. Our observations above are what we saw in one
setup, not established facts; check them against what you find.
