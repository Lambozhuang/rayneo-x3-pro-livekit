# Two lines: the Decisions API reads the moments, the brain's model judges (2026-11-05, evening)

This note records the structure agreed on the evening of 2026-11-05, after the frozen path (tag `freeze-2026-11-05`, a brain on gpt-6.1-terra looking back to back) had been judged at the ceiling of its speed and after `eval/decisions-frames/` had measured what the Decisions API reads off one frame: the moment a frame shows, reliably; whether a placement is right, not at all.

## The structure

The brain's line stays as it is: its own conversation with the vision model, one look after the other, the voice's unfinished line in each look, the Progress cut-in, the record of the build. It says what is placed right or wrong and how to fix it, moves the progress, answers questions about the desk, and no longer confirms the brick in the wearer's hand, which the second line does.

A second line sends every frame to the Decisions API, one request after the other, each about half a second: the frame at 720 px, the developer text written from the task (the scene, the direction words, the build's bricks, the steps placed, the current step), and five questions, the plate in view, a hand on the plate, a brick held, which of the task's bricks it is, the step's brick on the plate with no hand on it.

The second line does two things with the answers and nothing else.

1. When a brick is held and the choice names the current step's brick above a threshold, GPT-Live gets one commentary, "Camera: yes, that's the one."; when it names another brick above the threshold, "Camera: not that one; the step wants the <brick>." A flag then holds the same line for a few seconds, so one pick-up gets one line and not one a frame. In between the thresholds it says nothing and leaves the brick to the brain's line.
2. When a hand on the plate has gone and the step's brick lies on the plate, the brain looks now, at the very frame the Decisions API just read, and a look of the brain's in flight whose frame is older is cancelled. The placement verdict then comes one brain look after the hand leaves instead of up to two.

The second line never judges a placement, never changes the record, and never sends a line per frame. The two lines do not arbitrate: the brick in the hand is the Decisions line's, the plate is the brain's.

## After the first home run (2026-11-06 00:03, the dog task)

The user held a dark red 2x6 at the step that wanted the red 2x4 and a tan 1x6 at the step that wanted the tan 2x6. The choice over the build's bricks named them red 2x4 and tan 2x6 at 0.92 to 1.00 and never "another brick"; asked the same frames afterwards (`eval/decisions-frames/home-bricks.toml`), the yes-or-no "the held brick is the current step's brick, the same colour and the same size" gave 0.87 to 0.95 for the dark red 2x6 and 0.03 to 0.92 for the tan 1x6, depending on the frame's size. The model reads a brick's hue, not its size. The user's ruling: the system is an instrument for measuring what a bad network does to the experience, not a product to perfect, so the brick line stays, as the yes-or-no, accepted as roughly right.

The same run changed three mechanics: the brick line is said only with the hand away from the plate, since with the hand on the plate the model read the brick placed as the brick held; one line per pick-up, the next only once the hand has read empty, since a timer had the same "yes" said seven times for one brick held for fifty seconds; and the brain's look is called on the hand leaving the plate, the frame before reading a hand on it and this one not, since "the step's brick is on the plate" alone read 0.9 twice on an empty plate. The frame each line or look is acted on is saved in the record (`moment<n>-s<step>.jpg`).

## After the home runs of the night (2026-11-06, 00:52 to 01:52)

Five more runs with the step's five bricks picked up one after the other settled the brick line's mechanics. One frame's reading drifts, the same brick reading as the step's at 0.9 and 0.33 a frame apart and its colour as tan and white, so the first line for a brick goes on the first frame that reads it, since speed is what the line is for, while saying again waits for three agreeing frames: another verdict, or another colour than the one the brick settled on in the three frames after its line, since the line's own frame misreads the colour ("none" with a brick held at 0.95), or the hand empty for three frames. The thresholds are 0.9 for "yes" and 0.4 for "not that one", two lines are at least two seconds apart, and the line goes as a commentary with a cut-in instruction before it while the voice is talking, since an instruction alone made the voice break off and say nothing. The last run (01:52) gave one line a brick, seven for seven, the frame answered 0.2 to 0.9 s after it was taken and the voice starting 0.7 to 1.3 s after the line.

Two things are measured and left as they are. The right brick reads as the step's at 0.75 to 0.89 for one to five frames before a frame reaches 0.9, so "yes" comes one to three seconds later than "not that one", whose frames read 0.0 to 0.07 at once; a threshold of 0.85 would be faster and would have called a yellow 1x4 the tan 1x6 (0.89). And the time from the line to the voice's first sound, 0.7 to 1.8 s, is GPT-Live's own; the glasses' link during these calls had 5 ms of audio round trip, 1 ms of jitter and no loss (the glasses' own link lines), so the network was not in it. The one way past GPT-Live's time is for the agent to play the two fixed lines as audio made in advance with the same voice and to tell GPT-Live by a thinking line what was said; that would also let the slice experiment set the Decisions answer's delay exactly. Not built.

## Open

Whether the second line should run at two or three frames a second rather than back to back, and whether the brain's own back-to-back looks can slow down once the hand's leaving starts a look, are both for after those runs.
