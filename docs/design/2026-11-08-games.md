# The study's tasks become two short games, and game B, place the brick, is built on the existing agent (2026-11-08)

This note records how the placement game of the user study is built into the agent and the glasses, and why it is built this way. What the game has to be is fixed outside the repo, in the study's `game-spec.md` and `study-design.md` (`OneDrive - Ericsson\QoE AIAR glasses\user-study\`); this note is the implementation's side of it.

## Why games

Four pilots with the five-step build showed that the build's own pace, the agent's talk, five steps and three to four minutes, hid the network's effect on the two interactions the study is about: getting immediate feedback on what one holds, and getting a visual check of what one has placed. On 2026-11-08 the user replaced the build with two games of one to one and a half minutes, each one interaction and one network factor: game A, pick the brick, under added round-trip delay, and game B, place the brick, under an uplink bandwidth cap. The full build stays as a warm-up outside the console. The user's rule for the implementation: the system stays as it is, a game is prompts and the joins between what exists, no new mechanism, no change of the screen, no tuning.

## What game B is

A blue 2x6 is fixed on the plate before the trial. Six times the glasses show a picture of where the red 2x4 goes against it, and the hint line under the dots says where to put it first, one row or one column off the target. The participant puts it there and takes the hand away; the plate is judged and the voice says it is off. They move it onto the target, hand away; the plate is judged and the voice says it is right. They lift it, and the next picture comes; the hint line says to lift it, since the next picture waits for that. With the sixth round's right the voice says the game is over, and the call ends by itself as a finished build's does. The participant need not speak. The six targets are one fixed set, in a different order in each of the six trial files (a Williams square), and the targets with their offsets are chosen so that the brick stays on the plate and against the anchor and the error is one stud: touching or a gap, ends or sides level or one off.

## How it is built

A trial is a task file like a build's (`backend/agent/guides/b-1` to `b-6`, numbers 101 to 106, so the glasses' picker and the token endpoint take them without change), with `game = "place"`, an `[anchor]` brick, and a step per round: the step's `brick` is the movable brick, its `place` the target as the judge is told it, its `offset` where the hint asks for the brick first, its `cells` what `render_pictures.py` draws, which for a game is the anchor and that round's target alone. The agent's `Build` counts rounds as it counts steps, so the step list, the progress, the picture and the end of the call all work as for a build, and the report to the console says how many rounds were done.

In the call (`gpt/agent.py`) a `PlaceGame` (`gpt/place.py`) stands where the brain stands, with the same surface, so nothing else in the wiring changes. The second line (`gpt/moments.py`) runs as it does in a build with one difference, it says no brick line: its text tells the Decisions model about the anchor and the movable brick, and its question "the current step's brick is on the plate with no hand on it", true on two frames in a row, calls `look_now` as it calls the brain's. The judge sends that frame's bottom square and this round's target to the vision model, in a conversation of one turn, so it has nothing of the rounds before and cannot learn that the first placement of a round is always off; its one tool is `verdict`, right, off or cannot_tell, with a sentence of what it saw for the labelling afterwards. The voice gets the verdict's line as a commentary, as it gets the brain's lines, and says it in its own words; the user decided against pre-made audio and fixed phrases, since the voice's wording and timing are part of what is measured. A right moves the round, which takes the picture off the glasses; when the second line's reading drops, the brick lifted, `lifted` sends the next round's picture and hint. A cannot_tell or a failed request has the second line rearmed two seconds later, so the same placement is judged again when the reading holds; there is no other fallback, by the user's decision: a trial that stalls at the bandwidth floor is a result.

The glasses show the hint line as the agent publishes it (`rayneo.hint`, `Build.hint`), in the place of the phase's words above the dots; the title, the step list (the rounds), the camera's square and the picture are as in a build. Two launch parameters are new: `-Game A|B` with `-Lab` makes the session a block of that game, the console's experiment run k naming the trial file k and its training run k the file 4 + k (`Lab.kt`); `-MaxKbps` caps the camera track's bitrate (`Uplink.kt`), so the frames the judge gets at 250, 150 and 100 kbit/s can be looked at at home, where there is no netem box, to set the study's bandwidth floor. The cap is the encoder's target, not a link cap: under a real cap WebRTC's estimate sets the bitrate below it, with queueing and loss on top, so a floor found this way is a little optimistic.

The record gets `round` (the picture and hint sent), `judge_sent` and `verdict` (with the model's time, usage, the sentence of what it saw and the saved frame `judge<n>-r<round>.jpg`), `step` and `build` as for a build, and the voice's `commentary`, `voice_delta` and `said` as before; the second line's `look_now` event holds the time the hand was read as away.

## What game A will need

The same frame: a task file per trial with the six colours in order, the Decisions text and question changed from "the current step's brick" to "the named colour", no plate in the questions, a state machine that names a colour, waits for the brick to be read as held, judges on two frames, flashes the dots and gives the voice its line, waits for the hand to be empty and names the next; after the sixth the hint "Now ask the agent which bricks you picked", and the call ends when the voice has answered. The hues, from the kit's 2x4 bricks and what the Decisions model has been measured to read: red, blue, green, purple, pink, white and light grey, seven, since black is out by the user's decision and no eighth hue of that size reads reliably in the data.

## To verify on the device

Whether the trigger comes only after the hand is away and once per placement; whether the judge tells a one-row and a one-column offset from the target at full bitrate, and at which cap it stops; how the voice says the three lines and how long after the verdict; whether the picture and the hint change at the right moments and the call ends after the sixth right; and whether a round ever stalls, which the record will show as a `look_now` without a `verdict` or a `verdict` of cannot_tell repeating.
