# The study's tasks become two short games, and game B, place the brick, is built on the existing agent (2026-11-08)

This note records how the placement game of the user study is built into the agent and the glasses, and why it is built this way. What the game has to be is fixed outside the repo, in the study's `game-spec.md` and `study-design.md` (`OneDrive - Ericsson\QoE AIAR glasses\user-study\`); this note is the implementation's side of it.

## Why games

Four pilots with the five-step build showed that the build's own pace, the agent's talk, five steps and three to four minutes, hid the network's effect on the two interactions the study is about: getting immediate feedback on what one holds, and getting a visual check of what one has placed. On 2026-11-08 the user replaced the build with two games of one to one and a half minutes, each one interaction and one network factor: game A, pick the brick, under added round-trip delay, and game B, place the brick, under an uplink bandwidth cap. The full build stays as a warm-up outside the console. The user's rule for the implementation: the system stays as it is, a game is prompts and the joins between what exists, no new mechanism, no change of the screen, no tuning.

## What game B is

A blue 2x6 is fixed on the plate before the trial. Six times the glasses show a picture of where the red 2x4 goes against it, and the hint line under the dots says where to put it first, one row or one column off the target. The participant puts it there and takes the hand away; the plate is judged and the voice says it is off. They move it onto the target, hand away; the plate is judged and the voice says it is right. They lift it, and the next picture comes; the hint line says to lift it, since the next picture waits for that. With the sixth round's right the voice says the game is over, and the call ends by itself as a finished build's does. The participant need not speak. The six targets are one fixed set, in a different order in each of the six trial files (a Williams square), and the targets with their offsets are chosen so that the brick stays on the plate and against the anchor and the error is one stud: touching or a gap, ends or sides level or one off.

## How it is built

A trial is a task file like a build's (`backend/agent/guides/b-1` to `b-6`, numbers 101 to 106, so the glasses' picker and the token endpoint take them without change), with `game = "place"`, an `[anchor]` brick, and a step per round: the step's `brick` is the movable brick, its `place` the target as the judge is told it, its `offset` where the hint asks for the brick first, its `cells` what `render_pictures.py` draws, which for a game is the anchor and that round's target alone. The agent's `Build` counts rounds as it counts steps, so the step list, the progress, the picture and the end of the call all work as for a build, and the report to the console says how many rounds were done.

In the call (`gpt/agent.py`) a `PlaceGame` (`gpt/place.py`) stands where the brain stands, with the same surface, so nothing else in the wiring changes. The second line (`gpt/moments.py`) runs as it does in a build with one difference, it says no brick line: its text tells the Decisions model about the anchor and the movable brick, and its question "the current step's brick is on the plate with no hand on it", true on two frames in a row, calls `look_now` as it calls the brain's. The judge sends that frame's bottom square and this round's target to the vision model, in a conversation of one turn, so it has nothing of the rounds before and cannot learn that the first placement of a round is always off; its one tool is `verdict`, right, off or cannot_tell, with a sentence of what it saw for the labelling afterwards. The voice gets the verdict's line as a commentary, as it gets the brain's lines, and says it in its own words; the user decided against pre-made audio and fixed phrases, since the voice's wording and timing are part of what is measured. A right moves the round, which takes the picture off the glasses; when the second line's reading drops, the brick lifted, `lifted` sends the next round's picture and hint. A cannot_tell or a failed request has the second line rearmed two seconds later, so the same placement is judged again when the reading holds; there is no other fallback, by the user's decision: a trial that stalls at the bandwidth floor is a result.

The glasses show the hint line as the agent publishes it (`rayneo.hint`, `Build.hint`), in the place of the phase's words above the dots; the title, the step list (the rounds), the camera's square and the picture are as in a build. Two launch parameters are new: `-Game A|B` with `-Lab` makes the session a block of that game, the console's experiment run k naming the trial file k and its training run k the file 4 + k (`Lab.kt`); `-MaxKbps` caps the camera track's bitrate (`Uplink.kt`), so the frames the judge gets at 250, 150 and 100 kbit/s can be looked at at home, where there is no netem box, to set the study's bandwidth floor. The cap is the encoder's target, not a link cap: under a real cap WebRTC's estimate sets the bitrate below it, with queueing and loss on top, so a floor found this way is a little optimistic.

The record gets `round` (the picture and hint sent), `judge_sent` and `verdict` (with the model's time, usage, the sentence of what it saw and the saved frame `judge<n>-r<round>.jpg`), `step` and `build` as for a build, and the voice's `commentary`, `voice_delta` and `said` as before; the second line's `look_now` event holds the time the hand was read as away.

## 2026-11-09: game B becomes build and report

The placement game was tried at home on 2026-11-08 at encoder caps of 250 and 100 kbit/s: the judge told a one-row offset right in all seven judgements, while in free play the same evening the brain's colours and stud counts degraded with the cap. A game that the bandwidth does not touch measures nothing, so the user replaced it: the participant builds one of the existing five-brick sheets from pictures on the glasses, in three batches of two, two and one brick, and after each batch asks the agent what it sees; the agent, which does not know the sheet, reports only the bricks added since its last report, each with its colour, its size and its place against a brick already on the plate, no overall shape and no guess, saying so for a field it cannot read; the participant's "next" moves the display to the next batch, so the trial moves on the participant's words and never on the vision; after the third batch the participant asks for a description of the whole build, which is not scored, and the call ends once the voice has given it. The reports are scored afterwards against the sheet.

Built as `gpt/report.py` on the free-play pieces: the glasses ask for the game with `-Game B` on any sheet (`&game=B` on the token request, `Build.game == "report"`), the brain watches as in free play with the reporting rules and no task text, the voice says "Let me look." and the report as it comes, and says one fixed sentence, "Here come the next bricks.", when the participant asks for the next ones; the game hears that sentence in the voice's transcript and shows the next batch's picture, `batch-<k>.png`, the bricks up to the batch's last with the batch's own outlined, which `render_pictures.py` now draws for every sheet. The hint line is one fixed text per batch, "Place these bricks, then ask the agent what it sees. When it has answered, say next." and for the last batch "Place this brick, then ask the agent what it sees. When it has answered, ask it to describe the whole build." (the user's wording of 2026-11-09 night, after two runs in which a hint that changed with the voice's turns was missed or stuck); the step list marks the batch's two steps (`rayneo.build.span`) and the batch picture outlines both bricks, each outline drawn inside its brick so that two touching bricks show two outlines (the first run of 2026-11-09 read a pole of two outlined bricks as one); the brain's line after that, the description, finishes the build in the agent's record, and the call ends as a finished build's does, the voice's silence after it and then `rayneo.call.over`, with the console's end_task and the double tap as the backstop. The second line does not run. The record has the question (`said`), the look with its saved frame (`watch_sent`), the report (`say`, `commentary`, `voice_delta`), `batch` for each picture and `report` for each hint change. The placement game's code and trial files stay.

## 2026-11-09, after the first lab pilot: both games revised

The commit messages of this day (c2cdd65 to a015e5b) and the names and notes of the tags `study-freeze-2026-11-10`, `-10b` and `-10c` call it 2026-11-10; it was Monday 2026-11-09, the date the commits themselves carry and the lab's records are named by (the run those messages call 2026-11-10 15:43 is `20261109-154306-glasses-d7c89ce2`).

The first full pilot (a colleague, 2026-11-09 morning) changed both games; the user's rule for the revision: no special cases, a state that cannot be judged is not judged, no timers, no re-asks, no change to the camera crop. Game A is two picks from five colours (grey out), a wrong brick allowed and judged as such, the second colour named at once after the first verdict, and at the end the participant asks what colour their first brick was; the voice answers from its conversation and the trial ends on that answer, read as the voice's first line after its own verdict turn that names one of the five colours, so nothing rests on the participant's words (three of four pilot runs had ended on the participant's talk about the brick in their hand). The next pick is judged only once the hand has read empty, so the brick still in the hand is not judged against the next colour. Game B is the whole sheet in one go from one picture of the figure (`figure.png`), the agent silent while the participant builds, then two questions: what it sees, answered by the brain with every brick's colour, size and place in its own words, and (from 2026-11-09 afternoon, in place of what the whole build looks like, first "last", then "first" the same day) which brick was placed first, which the brain answers from what it saw while the participant built, colour and size, or says it does not know; the brain's first line is taken as the description and its second as that answer, whatever was asked, and the second ends the trial. The batches and the "next" are gone.

The same afternoon the game was made to move on the brain's own word: its say in this game carries `answering`, what_is_on_the_plate, the second question's label or something_else, the description switches the hint, the second answer ends the trial and anything else changes nothing, so a question the participant puts in between no longer ends the trial early (2096b12). In the evening, after the runs of 14:49 and 15:43, the second question became in what order the bricks were placed, which the brain answers as it saw them appear, bricks that came in one look said to have appeared together, so a build of three bricks laid between two looks still has an answer where "which came first" had none; the description leaves the order out, since it is the second question; and the brain's `tell` tool is gone from the game, since in the 14:49 run a tell about the first brick reached the voice as a silent fact before the question was asked and the voice answered the second question from it without the brain's say, which is what ends the trial. ## 2026-11-10: game A judges the next pick once the judged brick is out of the frame, and shows its hint from the start

In the run 20261110-152453 the user went from the red brick to a white one and then a blue one without the hand reading empty in any frame, and the two stayed unjudged for 15 s, until one frame read the hand as empty. The next pick is now judged once a frame no longer shows the brick just judged: the hand reads empty (held below 0.5, as before), or the colour read is none, or it is another colour than the one read in the verdict's frame. That frame arms the pick and the frames after it are read for it, as before. The purpose stands: the brick still in the hand from the last pick is not judged against the next colour. One frame that reads the held brick's colour differently arms the pick too, and the brick still held is then judged against the next colour; in that run the 10 frames after the red verdict all read red, the 25 of the white brick white and the 7 of the blue one blue, and the colour read changed only as a brick came or went. A first pick that was wrong and of the colour named second stays unjudged while it is held on, and is judged once it has left the hand and is held again. The hint line is published at the start of the trial, before the greeting, as game B's is; it had come up after the greeting, 6.5 s into that run. Replayed through `gpt/pick.py` on that run's 73 recorded readings, with no request to a model, the game arms at the frame of 15.35 s and gives the second verdict at 15.98 s, not blue for the white brick, where the run gave it at 31.5 s for the blue one. There is no timer and no re-ask, and the thresholds, the prompts, the crop, game B and the glasses are untouched.

## Game A, pick the brick (built 2026-11-09, superseded above)

A task file per trial (`guides/a-1` to `a-6`, numbers 201 to 206, `game = "pick"`), a step per pick with the brick's colour, four of the tray's six colours, no two trials the same four or the same order; the hues are red, blue, purple, pink, white and light grey, from the kit's 2x4 bricks and what the Decisions model has been measured to read, black out by the user's decision and green out after the first runs of 2026-11-09, the kit's green being a teal that misled. The hint line reads "Listen for the colour, pick that brick up and hold it up. Put it back once the agent has confirmed it." through the picks (the user's wording). `gpt/pick.py` stands where the brain stands and no brain runs: after the greeting it gives the voice "Pick up the red brick.", sends every frame to the Decisions model with three questions, a brick held in the air, the held brick is the named colour, which colour it is, records the first frame read as held, and on two frames in a row at `MOMENTS_YES_AT` or above or at `MOMENTS_NOT_AT` or below flashes the dots green or red (`rayneo.agent`) and gives the voice "That's the red one." or "That's not the red one."; a brick held five seconds without a verdict is "none", the voice says it cannot tell and asks for it to be put back; when the hand has read empty for three frames the next colour follows. After the fourth verdict the hint reads "Now ask the agent which bricks you picked."; the wearer's next turn is taken as the question and finishes the build, the voice answers from its own conversation (a hand-over it makes all the same is closed with the list the game kept), and the call ends as a finished build's does. The record has `name`, `pick_held`, `pick_frame`, `pick_verdict` (with the frame saved) and `pick_question`, and the voice's `voice_delta` for when it starts speaking each line.
